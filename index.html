13 recent works, video first

Each card covers what the work does, an analogy, and why it matters for inspection. Videos are embedded from each paper's official project page and load only when you press play; the tabs underneath switch clips.

SONIC

Science Robotics

NVIDIAONE-G builds on this

Treats motion tracking as a foundation task that scales: the policy grows to 42M parameters and learns from 700 hours of motion capture, and the same policy takes VR teleoperation, human video and VLA models as input.

Analogy
A cerebellum that has watched 700 hours of human movement. Whatever sits on top, a joystick, a video or a large model, only has to say which motion comes next.
For inspection
Squatting, kneeling and crawling, all common on inspection rounds, are shown on the real robot, and an operator can take over through VR.
  • 42M parameters
  • 700 h of motion capture
  • One policy, many inputs

PaperProject pageCode

Any2Any

arXiv 2026

LimX DynamicsONE-G builds on this

First aligns the kinematic interfaces of the source and target humanoids, then adds LoRA only to the modules most sensitive to dynamics. With about 1% of the compute and data, it moves SONIC from the Unitree G1 to the LimX Oli and Luna.

Analogy
An experienced driver switching cars: the skill stays, they only need to get used to the new throttle and brakes.
For inspection
Your own inspection humanoid doesn't have to be trained from scratch, and the same idea, adapting only the dynamics part, could absorb the extra mass of a gear backpack.
  • ~1% of compute and data
  • Trains ~5% of parameters
  • Tested on 4 humanoids

PaperProject pageCode

SuperMimic

CoRL 2026

UC BerkeleyONE-G builds on this

Fits one differentiable world model to simulator data and trains the policy by backpropagating through it. On a single GPU it beats the return of the released SONIC checkpoint in about 3 hours (SONIC trained for 7 days on 128 GPUs), and it runs zero-shot on a real G1.

Analogy
A gymnast rehearsing in their head: first build a feel for the physics, then practise in imagination, and use the real simulator only to keep that feel calibrated.
For inspection
A lab with one GPU can iterate on new inspection skills. The paper also shows the world model predicting a slide when the floor turns slippery.
  • 1 GPU
  • ~3 hours
  • 129k motions

PaperProject pageTry it in the browser

BeyondMimic

Science Robotics 2026

UC Berkeley, Stanford

Open-sources a training recipe that reliably runs highly dynamic motions such as cartwheels, jumping spins and sprints on real hardware, and uses guided diffusion to compose those motions into tasks like navigation and obstacle avoidance.

Analogy
Master each hard move first, then string the moves into a motion language that can carry out a task.
For inspection
The open recipe can be reproduced as is, and guided diffusion fits chaining “walk to the checkpoint, crouch, inspect” into one task.
  • Fully open recipe
  • Guided diffusion
  • Real-robot cartwheels

PaperProject pageCodeFull video (YouTube)

Humanoid-GPT

CVPR 2026

Tsinghua, Galbot, Beihang, et al.

Trains many RL experts on 2 billion frames of retargeted motion, then distills them into one GPT-style causal Transformer. It tracks unseen, highly dynamic motions zero-shot and reports scaling laws for data and model size.

Analogy
A large language model for movement: feed it all the human motion you can find, and the bigger it gets, the better it generalizes.
For inspection
The project page has real-robot demos of security patrol and disinfection work, both close to inspection duty.
  • 2B frames of motion
  • Transformer
  • <1.5 ms inference on an RTX 4090

PaperProject pageCode

BFM-Zero

ICLR 2026

CMU, Meta

Uses unsupervised RL to put motions, goal poses and rewards into one shared latent space, so a single policy can track motions, reach goal poses and optimize rewards without retraining.

Analogy
Instead of memorizing moves, it learns a map of what the body can do. Give it a motion, a goal or a reward, and it finds the matching spot on the map.
For inspection
It shows few-shot adaptation to a 4 kg payload on the torso and a natural recovery after being pulled to the ground, much like carrying gear and getting bumped on site.
  • Zero-shot, many tasks
  • Unsupervised RL
  • 4 kg payload adaptation

PaperProject pageCodeFull video (YouTube)

GMRRetargeting Matters

ICRA 2026

Stanford

Measures how retargeting quality affects tracking policies. Its open-source real-time retargeter supports more than a dozen humanoids, including wheeled ones, and Any2Any uses it to prepare training data for each robot.

Analogy
Tailoring for robots: rework a human motion into something the robot's body can actually do. How well it fits decides how well everything downstream learns.
For inspection
One inspection motion can be adapted to different robots quickly, and it can pull human motion out of ordinary monocular video.
  • Real time
  • Over a dozen humanoids
  • Video input

PaperCode

OmniRetarget

ICRA 2026 Best Paper

Amazon FAR, MIT, UC Berkeley, et al.

Uses an interaction mesh to keep the contacts and spatial relations between robot, objects and terrain during retargeting. With very little reward design it trains loco-manipulation and scene-interaction policies, and it can expand one demonstration across robots, terrains and objects.

Analogy
Copy more than the pose: keep where the hand rests and where the foot lands.
For inspection
Contact-rich data such as climbing onto platforms and carrying boxes is the groundwork for opening cabinets and getting over obstacles.
  • ICRA Best Conference Paper
  • Interaction mesh
  • Open dataset

PaperProject pageCodeDatasetFull video (YouTube)

ASAP

RSS 2025

CMU, et al.

Pre-trains in simulation, then trains a delta action model on real-robot data to make up for the dynamics gap, and fine-tunes the policy with that model plugged back into the simulator.

Analogy
Practise in the simulator, then use what the real robot recorded about its own errors to correct the simulator until it behaves like the real thing.
For inspection
Feeding real-robot errors back into simulation is essential for an inspection robot that has to run reliably for a long time.
  • Delta action model
  • Sim-to-real alignment
  • Open code

PaperProject pageCodeFull video (YouTube)

MOSAIC

arXiv 2026

BAAI (Beijing Academy of AI), et al.

Generalist trackers often get shaky during long teleoperation sessions on a real robot. MOSAIC adds a residual adapter trained on a small amount of device-specific data, and the adapters for PICO VR and Noitom motion capture are both open-source.

Analogy
The generalist tracker is the trunk; each new teleoperation device just plugs in a small adapter.
For inspection
A direct reference for remote takeover when something goes wrong on site. Code, models and dataset are all open.
  • Residual adapter
  • PICO and Noitom
  • Open models and data

PaperProject pageCodeDataset

TWIST2

ICRA 2026

Amazon FAR, Stanford, et al.

Pairs a PICO 4U VR headset with a robot neck that costs about $250 for portable, mocap-free whole-body teleoperation and data collection, about 100 demonstrations in 15 minutes. The system and the dataset are open-source.

Analogy
Put on the headset and you are inside the robot: cheap, portable, and quick to pile up whole-body demonstrations.
For inspection
The same setup could collect demonstrations on an inspection site in bulk, building up the data for future autonomous policies.
  • $250 robot neck
  • 100 demos in 15 min
  • Open system and data

PaperProject pageCodeDatasetVideo (Bilibili)

ResMimic

arXiv 2025

Amazon FAR, et al.

Starts from a general motion-tracking policy trained on large-scale human motion and learns a lightweight residual policy for object interaction, carrying boxes and lifting chairs with the whole body on a real G1.

Analogy
The master, a general tracker, sets the overall movement; the apprentice, a residual policy, only corrects how to line up with the object and how hard to push.
For inspection
Opening a cabinet, turning a valve or carrying a toolbox is also general motion plus a fine correction.
  • Two-stage residual
  • Whole-body contact
  • Open code

PaperProject pageCodeFull video (YouTube)

mjlab

arXiv 2026

UC Berkeley, et al.

Environments and tasksIsaac Lab-style API
GPU-parallel physicsMuJoCo Warp
uvx --from mjlab --refresh demo

Keeps Isaac Lab's modular environment API but runs on MuJoCo Warp GPU simulation underneath. It installs with one command and ships reference tasks for velocity tracking and motion imitation.

Analogy
A ready-to-use training ground: install it and a G1 can start learning to walk and imitate.
For inspection
New students can run motion imitation in their first week, on MuJoCo Warp, the same simulator SuperMimic uses.
  • One-command install
  • MuJoCo Warp
  • Training needs an NVIDIA GPU

A

Give a general controller a new body

Difficulty: intermediate

Transfer to inspection robots and adapt to payloads

Transfer a general tracker trained on the G1, such as SONIC, to an inspection humanoid at low cost, and adapt it to the mass and center-of-mass shift a gear backpack brings.

Good fit if you enjoy: math and algorithms, real robots and experiments

The question
How do you align different joint layouts? When the payload changes, which modules need adapting, and can the policy adapt online?
Read first
SONICAny2AnyGMRBFM-Zero
Month one
  1. Run the open-source Any2Any code and reproduce the simulated transfer of SONIC to a new robot
  2. Load the robot with backpacks of different weights in simulation and measure how tracking degrades
  3. Compare LoRA adaptation with full fine-tuning on quality and cost
Possible outcomes
A payload- and embodiment-aware transfer method, plus before-and-after results for walking, squatting and kneeling on the real robot.
B

A world-model tracker you can train on one GPU

Difficulty: challenging

Push further along the SuperMimic route

Replace most simulator sampling with a differentiable world model, so a lab can train and iterate on new inspection skills with a single GPU.

Good fit if you enjoy: math and algorithms

How do you keep world-model error in check when contact and friction change suddenly, as on a wet floor? Can terrain and payload become inputs to the world model?
Read first

5

Getting started in month one

Whichever topic you choose, the first four weeks can follow the same path: get things running, make some data, then choose a direction with real questions in hand.

  1. W1

    Learn the basics

    Read SONIC and BeyondMimic, run G1 motion imitation in mjlab, and try the SuperMimic web demo.

  2. W2

    Make some data

    Retarget LAFAN1 motions to the G1 with GMR, then film yourself squatting to read a gauge, extract the motion from the video and retarget it.

  3. W3

    Train and evaluate

    Train a tracking policy for that motion in mjlab, run a sim-to-sim check in MuJoCo and log the failure cases. Read ASAP and MOSAIC.

  4. W4

    Choose a topic

    Read the 2–3 papers behind the topic you like, then write a one-page proposal: the problem, the approach, and how you would test it in an inspection setting.

Glossary

The Chinese term is shown next to each entry, for talking with labmates.

Whole-body control (WBC)全身运控
Coordinating the legs, torso and arms at once, so the robot keeps its balance while it moves.
Motion tracking运动跟踪
Given a reference motion, the policy outputs joint commands so the robot follows it as closely as possible.
Retargeting重定向
Converting human motion into motion the robot's joints can execute.
Reinforcement learning (RL)强化学习
Improving a policy by trial and error in simulation, guided by rewards.
Sim-to-real仿真到真机
Deploying a policy trained in simulation on a real robot, and closing the gap between the two.
Cross-embodiment transfer跨本体迁移
Reusing a policy trained on one robot on another robot with a different body.
World model世界模型
A model that predicts what happens next, so a policy can practise inside it, in imagination.
Residual learning残差学习
Learning a small correction on top of an existing policy's output.
LoRA低秩适配
A fine-tuning method that trains a few low-rank parameters and leaves the original model mostly untouched.
VLA视觉-语言-动作模型
Vision-language-action model: it looks, takes an instruction, then outputs actions.
Teleoperation遥操作
A person controls the robot in real time through VR, a mocap suit or a similar device.
Behavior foundation model (BFM)行为基础模型
A pre-trained general behavior policy that can be prompted in different ways to do different tasks.
